Claresholm must select 6 key priority sectors (industries) and 25 occupations (job offers) to include in the program for a given year, and we have the opportunity to review and adjust these selections each year. In 2024 and 2025 a similar survey was sent out to inform the selections for 2025 and 2026. We are now running a survey to inform the priority selections for 2027. This program was launched on April 29th, 2025. It is an employer driven program and its purpose is to help Claresholm and area employers fill priority labour shortages with eligible and skilled workers who have a strong intention to live and work in Claresholm long term. We have a total of 36 allocations for 2026. 

The Town of Claresholm has been accepted to participate in the Rural Community Immigration Pilot (RCIP). RCIP is an economic immigration pilot and pathway to permanent residency aimed toward filling important labour shortages in Claresholm. RCIP is designed for workers who have an intention to reside in the community long term, and if approved, are granted the ability to apply for permanent residency as well as an LMIA-exempt work permit. Claresholm is participating with a 25km boundary which includes Granum and Stavely.

This program is now launched in Claresholm and we are now accepting applications from eligible employers to become designated. Employers will need to be within one of the designated industries and will need to be hiring for an occupation listed within that specific industry. We will designate and work directly with employers in the community who may hire foreign workers to fill the priority labour market needs, or who may support existing employees already filling priority labour needs on a pathway to permanent residency. 

This pilot is designed to address the unique needs of the Claresholm area and use new approaches to immigration by targeting specific local labour market needs. When utilizing one of the NOC codes below, it must be a genuine and accurate fit to the job a candidate will perform and describe the main role of the job, it must also be consistent with the past and current hiring practices of an employer. Rural Community Immigration Pilot Information

Attention Candidates

If you have any concerns about the legitimacy of a job offer, please contact us at eda@claresholm.ca. Job offers can only be provided by the employer themselves, so you should speak with them directly. We can verify that you who you are talking to is actually the employer. If you have not met with the employer for an interview in person or via online meeting, you should not trust the legitimacy of the job offer. An immigration consultant who does not put you in direct contact with the employer should not be trusted. 

Intake Schedule

We will issue three (3) allocations per month to ensure we are spreading our allocations out over the year, with the opportunity for excess allocations to be utilized at the end of the year. Intake periods will be from the first of each month to the second last Tuesday of each month, with Committee decisions taking place on the Thursday following that (see calendar for December exception). Community Recommendations will be issued to highest scoring candidates two business days following the meeting and decision.

If your application is not fully completed (as in all necessary forms and documents submitted and the interview process is completed) before the cutoff date, your application will be considered at the next months meeting.

2026 Scoring Grid

Each Candidate will be assessed against the scoring grid, and need at least 50 point to be eligible.

Additionally, employers will only be able to use the Pilot when their industry and the occupation(s) that they are hiring for is included within the priority industry and occupation list for a given year (these will be reviewed and possibly adjusted for each new year). Information for Employers

The Claresholm RCIP will designate employers with labour needs that align with and support community identified priority sectors and occupations. These employers are key to the success of the immigration pilot as they will directly recruit newcomers to fill labour market needs and settle in the community.

Below is the process of this program:

  1. Claresholm EDC sets priority sectors/occupations for the year.
  2. Employers interested in the pilot complete the required training, apply to the community for designation and meet with Coordinator.
  3. Communities designate employers in priority sectors/occupations and who meet other designation requirements.
  4. Designated employers recruit in Canada (temporary residents) or overseas to fill job vacancies, or may support existing temporary worker employers through the program
  5. Employers assess prospective candidates or existing employees, vet them against minimum federal eligibility requirements, and make job offers to best candidate.
  6. With job offer, employer applies to community for recommendation on behalf of candidate within an intake period.
  7. EDC assesses candidate against federal eligibility requirements.
  8. Community issues recommendation to candidate and candidate applies to IRCC for permanent residence.
  9. IRCC assesses candidates against eligibility criteria and admissibility requirements.
  10. Candidate can apply to IRCC for work permit (optional).
  11. IRCC makes final decision on work permit and permanent residence application. If eligible, candidate obtains permanent residence.

Employers must complete and submit an application for designation to the EDC for approval. The EDC will determine whether employers meet the designation criteria below:

  • the business is genuine and has been in continuous, active operation under the same management for two years within the community,
  • the employer carries on business in at least one priority occupation and at least 75 percent of work is to be performed within the community;
  • the employer has completed intercultural competency training;
  • the employer has completed mandatory onboarding training;
  • the employer agrees to support the settlement of each Principal Applicant and their accompanying family member(s), including by facilitating access to the settlement and social service supports;
  • the employer is not in violation of employer compliance regimes under the IRPA or IRPR;
  • the employer is not in contravention of employment standards and occupational health and safety legislation.
  • The employer is not:
    • a consulate;
    • an employer referred to in paragraphs 200(3)(g.1) or (h) of the Regulations;
    • a business that recruits individuals in order to establish a pool of candidates who are intended to be transferred or contracted to other businesses; or
    • a business in which a majority of shares or other ownership interests is held, either individually or collectively, by the foreign national or their spouse or common-law partner or that is controlled, directly or indirectly, by the foreign national or their spouse or common-law partner
      • Note: the intent of this provision is to exclude from designation any foreign national with a business who is seeking to recommend themselves through the pilot.
        a business owned by a Representative, who is a person referred to in subsection 91(2) of IRPA.
  • The employer will need to provide evidence that they have been consistently and actively trying to hire local candidates and have not been able to fill available positions with Canadian citizens or permanent residents

The RCIP program can be utilized to provide a pathway to permanent residence for existing temporary foreign workers already hired and working for the company, or can be used by employers to provide an offer of employment to a candidate who will fill priority labor vacancies within their business.

  • Provide evidence of attempt to hire outside of RCIP: All employers are required to provide evidence that they actively attempted to hire a Canadian citizen or Permanent resident for the position. This can be done through evidence such as original job advertisement, number and proof of Candain Citizen or PR resumes received, and an explanation as to why these where not selected, and the TFW was selected.
  • Notify the Coordinator of whether or not you are hiring: All designated employers will be listed on the website once they are designated. To mitigate the amount or resumes a business gets when thney are not hiring, beside each designated employer, it will either say “currently Hiring” or Not Currently Hiring”. To support this, designated employers who are actively hiring for a vacant position must notify the Coordinator that they are hiring. 
  • Posting to Canada Job Bank: Designated employers who are actively hiring for a vacant position must post their job posting to Canada Job Bank as well as any other posting methods of their choice (keeping in mind that they will need to provide evidence that they considered a Canadian Citizen or Permanent Resident for the position). This will allow the Coordinator to direct interested applicants to this site for information on available RCIP job opportunities.

The Economic Development Committee will make decisions on all employers’ application for designation. But prior to your application going to the committee, a meeting will be held between employers and the Coordinator to discuss the application package, assess employer commitment and eligibility, and answer any final questions.

  • Must meet with the Coordinator prior to Designation: Once the complete Employer designation application has been submitted to the Coordinator and reviewed for basic eligibility criteria, the Coordinator will reach out to the employer arrange a time for an in person meeting. This must be completed before the application can be taken to the EDC for final decision.

As a designated employer, you are responsible for supporting the settlement of foreign workers in their new workplace and community. This means connecting foreign workers with settlement programs and services in the community, and making the workplace a welcoming environment.  You are also responsible to keep in contact with the RCIP Coordinator and provide updates on staff (i.e. received work permit, received PR. arriving in Claresholm, quit, fired, promoted, new job in town, left town, etc.). More information on your responsibilities as a designated employer under the RCIP program are provided through the onboarding training and the meeting held with the RCIP Coordinator.

Employers within the selected priority industries and occupations and otherwise eligible according to the above criteria must email eda@claresholm.ca to receive the employer application package and training module links. Include in this email:

To request the EMPLOYER application package (for EMPLOYERS only) you can email eda@claresholm.ca. Include in this email:

  • Your business name
  • Physical address within the Claresholm RCIP boundaries
  • How long you have been operating out of this location (should be a minimum of 2 years)
  • The priority industry your business falls into
  • The main activities of your business
  • Which positions you wish to hire for
  • For how long, through what methods, and to what degree of success you have been hiring LOCALLY for these positions (Note: you must be able to prove that you have made recent and genuine attempts to hire locally)
  • Why and how you intend to utilize RCIP

Emails that do not include ALL of these items will not be responded to. Employers that do not meet the minimum requirements of the program will not receive the employer application package. Receiving the application package and training links does not guarantee that you will become a designated employer.

Information for Skilled Workers

In order to participate in the RCIP program interested applicants will need to first secure a eligible* job offer from a designated business in Claresholm or from an employer in priority sectors/occupations who meet other designation requirements and may become designated. This is an employer driven program which means employers handle their own hiring process, as a candidate it is your responsibility to contact employers and secure a job offer. The CEDC and Coordinator do not play any role in this.

*Eligible job offers are occupations included within the priority industry and occupation list above. Occupation are specific to the industries they are listed under. Industries or occupations not listed above are not eligible for this program and industries cannot use this program to hire candidates in occupations that are not listed under their specific industry.

To meet the federal eligibility requirements as outlined in the Ministerial Instructions with respect to the Rural Community Immigration Class, principal applicants must meet the following federal eligibility requirements:

  • Education:
    • have, at minimum, completed a Canadian secondary school diploma or the equivalent foreign credential, as evidenced by an Educational Credential Assessment from a designated organization or designated professional body
  • Work experience:
    • have accumulated at least one year of full-time work experience, or the equivalent in part-time work, in an occupation that is listed in category 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 of the National Occupational Classification (NOC) Training, Education, Experience and Responsibilities (TEER) matrix, within the previous three years prior to applying for permanent residence
      • Note: There is a work experience exemption for local graduates, however this does not apply to Claresholm as we have no qualifying schools within our RCIP boundaries. 
  • Job offer:
    • have a genuine job offer in the community from an employer that carries on business in the community. The job offer must be full-time, non-seasonal, for an indeterminate period (no end date), in an occupation that is listed in category 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 or 5 of the TEER matrix for which the applicant has related work experience and at a wage that is above or within the range of wages for that particular occupation in Job Bank, unless the EDO has established its own acceptable wage ranges above the minimum wage, informed by the local labour market.
  • Language proficiency:
    • For RCIP: have met the minimum score required under the Canadian Language Benchmark (CLB) in English or the Niveaux de competence linguistique canadien (NCLC) in French according to the TEER job offer by taking a designated language test
  • Intent to reside:
    • Have demonstrated intent to reside in the community
  • Settlement funds:
    • in the case of a foreign national who is not already legally working in Canada at the time of application for permanent residence, they must show they have sufficient funds to settle in Canada according to their family size (this includes all family members accompanying or not accompanying, whether they are Canadian citizens/permanent residents or not). Settlement funds are based on 50% of the low-income cut-off for rural areas.
    • EDOs are not required to assess settlement funds, just the attestation from the candidate confirming they have settlement funds available (IRCC will assess and verify settlement funds). Note: the proof of funds amount is updated on a yearly basis.
  • Valid temporary resident status:
    • in the case of a foreign national who is in Canada when they submit their permanent residence application, they must have temporary resident status
      • Note: Claresholm requires this to be in the form of a Work Permit

Once an interested applicant meets the eligibility requirements and has an eligible job offer from a designated employer, they can work with their employer to prepare the application package, the employer will submit this to the EDC on the applicants behalf. Specific links and more information about these requirements can be found on IRCC’s website.

Here is an overview of the process:
  1. The RCIP Review Committee (subcommittee of the Claresholm EDC) sets priority sectors/occupations for the year.
  2. Employers interested in the pilot complete the required training, apply to the community for designation and meet with Coordinator.
  3. The RCIP Review Committee designates employers in priority sectors/occupations and who meet other designation requirements, in accordance with the intake schedule.
  4. Designated employers recruit in Canada (temporary residents) or overseas to fill job vacancies, or support existing temporary worker employees through the program. Candidates interested in obtaining a job offer must contact or apply to designated employers directly. All employers hiring through RCIP are required to list job offers on Canada Job Bank, candidates can cross-reference job postings there with the designated employer list and apply that way.
  5. Employers assess prospective candidates or existing employees, vet them against minimum federal eligibility requirements, and make job offers to the best candidate.
  6. The employer requests the Application Package from the Coordinator, then works with the employee/candidate to complete it and gather all documents, and submits it to the coordinator for review on behalf of the candidate within an intake period.
  7. The Coordinator reviews the application package, assesses candidates against federal eligibility requirements, and meets with eligible candidates.
  8. The RCIP Review Committee issues recommendations to candidates in accordance with the intake schedule and scoring grid, and candidate applies to IRCC for permanent residence.
  9. IRCC assesses candidates against eligibility criteria and admissibility requirements.
  10. Candidate can apply to IRCC for a work permit (optional).
  11. IRCC makes final decision on work permit and permanent residence application. If eligible, candidate obtains permanent residence.

Settlement Services

Willow Creek Immigrant Services

WCIS provides settlement and integration services, all for free. There is much to learn when you and your family first arrive in Claresholm and area. They assist with your immediate needs for housing, schools, health care and more. Throughout the year, they also provide workshops and information sessions to give you more knowledge about how to make the region of Willow Creek your home.

Welcoming Claresholm Committee

A committee comprised of community members that strive to create a welcoming town where newcomers feel included and connected, with the ability to participate within the community as they choose. No matter where you were born or why you choose to be in Claresholm, you are important to our community and we are glad that you are here!

Meetings are held once a month at the Claresholm Town Office Youth and Community Room, the committee often participates in events such as Winterfest, Canada Day and Spread the World, Fair Days, Welcoming Week, Old Fashioned Christmas and Cozy Corner, and we often hold our own events such as pot lucks, BBQ’s, holiday gatherings, and training sessions. Program or Workplace Abuse

Employer and Consultant Fees are Illegal

An employer, or a consultant working on behalf of the employer, cannot request or accept any money from the candidate in exchange for providing a job offer or a job interview. As a candidate you do not need to pay an employer, if an employer is requesting money for a job offer or interview, please report this to eda@claresholm.ca. If, as a candidate, you hire an immigration consultant or lawyer, they cannot charge you for a job offer or for fees outside of what is described in your contract. Learn about the use of a representative here.

PLEASE contact me if you are experiencing abuse or fraud from your employer or if a prospective employer is asking you for money for a job offer. You can email me at eda@claresholm.ca, phone me at 403-468-8043, or visit me at the Town Office at 111 55 Ave West. There are ways to report abuse and fraud (anonymously if desired) to IRCC and you can apply for a Vulnerable Open Work Permit. Additionally, if your employer is de-designated due to fraud or abuse, your PR application will not be withdrawn but will be put on pause and you will have an opportunity to find a new job offer in Claresholm to keep your PR application in process.

There are solutions and reporting is essential to ensure this program runs with integrity, which is our greatest priority.

RCIP is not for everyone. There are other pathways. 

The Alberta Advantage Immigration Program (AAIP) is the province’s economic immigration program. It works in collaboration with the Government of Canada through Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC). Foreign workers, international students and others with the right skills, experience and education can apply to the  AAIP for a nomination through the various streams. The AAIP recognizes and nominates people for permanent residence who have the skills and experience the Alberta economy needs, and the Government of Canada makes the final decision to approve applications for permanent residence.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I qualify for the Post Secondary Graduate Work Experience Exemption?

No, Claresholm does not qualify for the work experience exemption for graduates, as you would have to graduate from a public post-secondary institution within our community, of which there are none.

Will job offers from industries and occupations not included in the priority industry and occupations list qualify?

No, only employers who fit within the priority industries are eligible for designation, and they can only provide job offers for one of the priority NOC codes. Therefore any job offer from an industry or NOC code not included on the priority industry and occupation list above will not qualify for RCIP.

What is included within Claresholm's 25km boundary?

Claresholm is participating in RCIP with a 25km boundary which includes Granum and the Town of Stavely. For exact details on which businesses fall within this boundary, you can utilize the interactive map found on IRCC’s RCIP webpage. Any businesses outside this 25KM boundary are not included in or eligible for Claresholm RCIP.
