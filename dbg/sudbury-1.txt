 Why Sudbury / Newcomers / RCIP and FCIP 
 A A A 
 Welcome. Bienvenue. Boozhoo.
 Thank you for your interest in the Greater Sudbury Rural Community Immigration Pilot (RCIP) and the Francophone Community Immigration Pilot (FCIP) Programs in Greater Sudbury, Ontario. The Sudbury RCIP and FCIP programs are delivered by the City of Greater Sudbury’s Economic Development division and funded by FedNor, the Greater Sudbury Development Corporation, and the City of Greater Sudbury.
 The RCIP and FCIP programs are a unique permanent residence pathway for international workers, aimed toward filling labour shortages in Greater Sudbury and surrounding communities. Designated Employers are expected to look locally to hire employees, including Canadian citizens and permanent residents, before recruiting foreign nationals. Both programs are designed for workers who have an intention to reside in the community long term, and if approved, are granted the ability to apply for permanent residency as well as an LMIA-exempt Work Permit.
 View the Greater Sudbury RCIP and FCIP programs community boundaries HERE .
 NEW – Requirement for Applicants on Maintained Status 
 Effective immediately, employers supporting applicants who are working under maintained status must provide additional information and supporting documentation to demonstrate that they remain eligible to work in Canada.
 Maintained status alone without additional supporting documentation is not sufficient for a candidate to be eligible to receive an RCIP / FCIP community recommendation. According to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), foreign nationals must meet all eligibility requirements for the specific work permit they have applied for. Failure to meet these requirements may result in the refusal of the work permit application which will likely also impact any subsequent applications that were submitted while the candidate was on maintained status.
 To ensure compliance with IRCC requirements, program staff must be satisfied that a candidate is legally authorized to work in Canada and eligible under the work permit for which they applied. Applicants on maintained status will therefore be required to explain the basis of their work permit application and provide documentation demonstrating that:
 Their maintained status is based on a work permit application submitted before their previous status expired;
 They meet the eligibility requirements of the work permit category under which they applied; and
 They satisfy the specific conditions and circumstances that support eligibility for that work permit.
 Providing clear details about the work permit application and the applicant&#8217;s continued eligibility will help ensure that applications can be assessed accurately and in accordance with IRCC requirements.
