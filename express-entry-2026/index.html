Express Entry 2026: pick the right NOC, see your new points.

Canada is adding a new Labour Market Integration factor to the Comprehensive Ranking System. It gives points for either your Canadian work experience or a qualifying job offer, whichever scores higher. Which points you get now depends heavily on your National Occupational Classification (NOC) code.

What changed

One new factor, two ways to score, and you keep the higher one

From November 3, 2026, IRCC will compare the points you could get from your Canadian work experience with the points from a qualifying job offer. You get whichever is higher. You can't add them together.

Route 1

Canadian work experience

Work-length points (the same points given today for Canadian experience in TEER 0 to 3 jobs) plus new extra points if at least 12 months of it was in an eligible high-wage occupation.

Step 1 · Choosing your NOC

Your duties decide your NOC, not your job title or your pay

Every point in the new factor flows from your NOC code. The high-wage tier is set by the national median wage of everyone in Canada working in that NOC, not by your own salary or job title. Getting the NOC right matters more than ever, and getting it wrong can cost you far more than points.

1

Start with what you actually do

List your real daily duties for each job: what you spend most of your time on, who you report to, and who reports to you. Your employer's reference letter should describe these same duties.

2

Match the lead statement and main duties

Search the official NOC 2021 site by keyword, then read the lead statement and main duties of each likely code. Pick the one where your duties match the lead statement and most of the main duties. Check the "exclusions" list too, which points to look-alike codes.

3

Then check the tier

Only after you've found the honest match, look it up in the checker below to see its high-wage tier. If your NOC isn't listed, your experience can still earn work-length points if it's TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3.

Reading a NOC code

21232

The second digit is the TEER level, which shows the training, education, experience and responsibilities the job usually needs. Code 21232 (software developers and programmers) is TEER 1.

Don't choose a NOC for the points

  • Title doesn't matter. A "Project Manager" who mostly codes may belong in a developer NOC. A "Manager" who mostly serves customers is likely not in a management NOC.
  • Salary doesn't change the tier. A well-paid job in an unlisted NOC earns no high-wage points. A lower-paid job in a listed NOC still does.
  • Officers check. When you apply for permanent residence, an officer compares your duties, reference letters and pay records against the NOC you claimed.
  • The risk is serious. Claiming a NOC that doesn't match your real duties can be treated as misrepresentation, which can mean a refusal and a five-year ban from applying.

Step 4 · Points from a job offer

Job offers count again, and the LMIA is gone

From November 3, 2026, these rules apply to job offers under Express Entry:

What's new

  • No LMIA is needed for a job offer to count for program eligibility or CRS points.
  • Having an LMIA gives no extra points and no priority.
  • The offer can be for an existing or a new position, with an existing or a new employer.
  • Points depend on the offer's NOC tier: 100, 70 or 50. A job outside the high-wage list earns 0 job offer points.

What the officer checks

Every job offer is still assessed when you apply for permanent residence. The officer must be satisfied it's genuine, including whether:

  • the employer is actively in business, can meet the terms of the offer, actually needs the role, and follows employment and recruitment laws
  • you're qualified and able to do the work, and likely to accept the offer and do the job
Federal Skilled Worker and Skilled Trades applicants: eligibility and CRS points are assessed separately. A valid job offer in any eligible TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 job can still help you meet program requirements even if it earns no CRS job offer points because it isn't on the high-wage list.

Questions

Frequently asked

Does my own salary decide whether my job is high-wage?

No. The tier depends on the median wage of everyone in Canada working in your NOC compared with the average median wage of the whole workforce. Your personal pay or job title doesn't change it.

Can I get points for both my experience and a job offer?

No. IRCC works out both and gives you the higher one. The maximum is 150 points if you're single and 140 through the experience route if your spouse or partner is coming with you. A job offer is worth up to 100 either way.

Do I still need an LMIA for my job offer?

Not from November 3, 2026. An LMIA isn't needed for eligibility or for points, and having one gives no extra points. An officer will still check that the job offer is genuine when you apply for permanent residence.

My NOC isn't on the list. Do I lose my Canadian experience points?

No. If your experience is in a TEER 0, 1, 2 or 3 job, you still get work-length points (up to 70 or 80). You just won't get the extra high-wage points, and a job offer in that NOC won't earn CRS job offer points.

What if the high-wage list changes after I'm invited?

IRCC keeps using the tier that applied on the day you received your Invitation to Apply, whether a job moves tier, is removed or is added. The list is reviewed using the last three years of wage data.

Can I change my NOC to get more points?

Only if the new NOC actually matches your duties better. Your NOC must reflect the work you really did, backed by reference letters. Claiming a NOC that doesn't match can be treated as misrepresentation, which can lead to refusal and a five-year ban. If you're unsure, talk to a licensed immigration consultant (CICC) or a lawyer.

Will I need to update my profile myself?

No, for the recalculation. IRCC will recalculate every existing profile automatically, which may take up to two weeks while draws are paused. You only need to add a job offer if you have one, using the details in the "Updating your profile" section.
